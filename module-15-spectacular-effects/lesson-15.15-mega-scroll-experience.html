Lesson 15.15 · Spectacular Effects

The Mega Scroll Experience

A single long-scroll page combining multiple spectacular effects in sequence. This lesson is about composition — how to layer aurora, typewriter, 3D tilt, staggered grids, glassmorphism, neon glow, and marquee effects without them clashing. Every effect wrapped in @supports, every animation silenced by prefers-reduced-motion.

Composition principles

Individual effects are impressive; a page that combines six of them without chaos is engineering. The key principles:

1. One hero effect per section

Each scroll section should have exactly one primary effect. The aurora is the hero backdrop, not the typewriter. The neon glow is the feature heading, not the content. When two effects compete for attention in the same viewport, both lose impact.

2. Transition tone between sections

Moving from a dark aurora hero to a bright grid reveal needs a gradient transition — a section that eases from dark to light. Abrupt tone shifts feel like separate pages stitched together. Gradual tone shifts feel like one continuous experience.

3. Performance budget

Running six simultaneous CSS animations burns GPU memory. The solution: use animation-timeline: view() where possible so effects only animate when visible. The aurora hero should pause or simplify when scrolled out of view. The marquee can run indefinitely because it is lightweight (only translate).

4. Reduced motion kills all decorative animation

One @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) block at the bottom disables every animation, transition, and scroll-driven effect. The page should still look good — just static. This is not optional; it is a requirement.

Composition checklist

Before shipping a multi-effect page, verify: (1) No two effects compete for attention in the same viewport. (2) Tone transitions between sections are gradual. (3) Every effect is in @supports where needed. (4) prefers-reduced-motion disables all decorative animation. (5) Scroll performance is smooth at 60fps. (6) The page tells a story — each section has a purpose, not just a technique.
