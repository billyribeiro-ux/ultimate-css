Lesson 15.4 · Spectacular Effects

Aurora / Northern Lights

A flowing, ethereal gradient animation that resembles the aurora borealis. Multiple layered gradients animated at different speeds with @property hue rotation, creating slow organic motion over 15-20 second cycles. This should be the most beautiful demo in the entire course.

Building the aurora

Real auroras are caused by charged particles exciting gas molecules at different altitudes — oxygen glows green and red, nitrogen glows blue and purple. We recreate this with multiple gradient layers, each moving independently.

The gradient stack

Three or four gradients layered with different blend modes and animation speeds. The key is low chroma — auroras glow, they do not saturate. OKLCH chroma values of 0.08-0.15 create that ethereal luminosity. Higher chroma looks artificial.

Smooth hue rotation with @property

By registering --aurora-hue-1 and --aurora-hue-2 as <number> types, we can animate them in keyframes. The gradient references these as oklch(60% 0.12 var(--aurora-hue-1)), creating a smooth, continuous hue shift that CSS cannot do with unregistered custom properties.

The frosted glass overlay

Text over the aurora uses backdrop-filter: blur(40px) to create a frosted glass panel. This ensures readability while letting the aurora glow through — the panel becomes part of the composition rather than hiding it.

Fallback pattern

The @property rule is required for smooth hue animation. Without it, custom properties are strings and cannot interpolate. Wrap the animated behavior in @supports (background: oklch(50% 0.1 0)) and provide a static gradient as a baseline. Browsers without @property still get a beautiful aurora — it just does not animate its hues.

/* Fallback: static aurora gradient for browsers without @property */
.l1 {
  background:
    radial-gradient(ellipse 80% 50% at 30% 20%,
      oklch(50% 0.12 150), transparent 70%),
    radial-gradient(ellipse 60% 40% at 70% 60%,
      oklch(40% 0.10 260), transparent 60%);
}

/* Enhanced: animated hue shift when oklch (and @property) is supported */
@supports (background: oklch(50% 0.1 0)) {
  .l1 {
    background:
      radial-gradient(ellipse 80% 50% at 30% 20%,
        oklch(50% 0.12 var(--hue1)), transparent 70%),
      radial-gradient(ellipse 60% 40% at 70% 60%,
        oklch(40% 0.10 var(--hue2)), transparent 60%);
    animation: drift1 18s ease-in-out infinite,
               hueShift 20s linear infinite;
  }
}

Demo — Aurora borealis

The Northern Lights

Charged particles from the solar wind collide with atmospheric gases, emitting photons at characteristic wavelengths. Oxygen produces green and red; nitrogen produces blue and purple. All recreated here in pure CSS.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Experiment with the hue values. Real auroras are predominantly green (hue ~150) and purple (hue ~290). Try shifting to warm hues (30-60) for a "sunset aurora" variant. Increase the chroma above 0.2 and notice how it loses the ethereal quality. Then try adding a fourth layer with a very slow 30-second animation for deep background movement.
