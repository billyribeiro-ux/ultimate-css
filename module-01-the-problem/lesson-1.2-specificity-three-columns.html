Lesson 1.2

Specificity: The Three-Column System

Specificity is not a number. It is a three-column score — A, B, C — and the columns do not carry over. Understanding why that matters prevents a category of bugs that trips up experienced developers.

The three columns

  • A — IDs. Each #id in a selector adds 1 to the A column.
  • B — classes, attributes, pseudo-classes. Each .class, [attr], or :hover adds 1 to B.
  • C — elements, pseudo-elements. Each div, h1, or ::before adds 1 to C.

The score is written as A,B,C. #main .btn:hover scores 1,2,0. button.primary[disabled] scores 0,3,1.

The zero cases

  • The universal selector * scores 0,0,0.
  • :where() scores 0,0,0 regardless of what it wraps. This is why the PE7 reset uses it.
  • Inline styles score above the system — conceptually 1,0,0,0.

The non-obvious rule: columns do not carry

0,0,11 does not beat 0,1,0. Eleven elements can never outweigh a single class. The columns are evaluated left to right — a higher A beats any B; a higher B beats any C — no matter how large the lower column is.

Common misconception

"More classes always wins." Walk through .a.b.c.d.e (0,5,0) losing to #id (1,0,0). Five classes lose to one ID.

Live demo — who wins?

.headline.headline.headline vs #title — who wins?

The #title selector scores 1,0,0. The triple .headline scores 0,3,0. Three classes cannot beat one ID. You should see indigo, not green.
