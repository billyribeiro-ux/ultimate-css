Lesson 13.3

color-mix(): Blend Any Two Colors

Chrome 111+, Firefox 113+, Safari 16.2+. Mix two colors at any ratio in any color space — no preprocessor functions, no JavaScript, no manual hex math. The result is a live, computed color.

The syntax

color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary) 70%, var(--color-surface)) blends 70% of the primary with 30% of the surface color. The percentages must add to 100% (or one can be omitted and the browser fills the remainder).

Why oklch is the best interpolation space

Blending in srgb produces muddy midpoints because sRGB is not perceptually uniform. Blending in oklch preserves vibrancy through the mix because lightness, chroma, and hue are independent perceptual axes. Always prefer in oklch unless you have a specific reason for another space.

Real-world use cases

Transparent overlays: color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-surface) 60%, transparent) creates a semi-transparent surface without hard-coding an alpha value.

Disabled states: color-mix(in oklch, var(--btn-bg) 40%, var(--color-surface)) fades a button to 40% presence — visually "grayed out" but derived from the original color, so it stays on-brand.

Surface tinting: color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-primary) 5%, var(--color-surface)) tints a background just enough to indicate selection without overwhelming the content.

Mini-exercise

Add a .card.hover variant with a background tinted at 4% brand — just enough to signal interaction. Then change --brand to a red (oklch(60% 0.22 25)) and confirm all three states still look cohesive. That is the advantage of deriving from tokens instead of hard-coding hex values.
