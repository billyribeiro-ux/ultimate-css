Lesson 5.7

Taming Third-Party CSS

Every vendor stylesheet you pull in — date pickers, charts, rich text editors, analytics dashboards — was written without any knowledge of your cascade. The PE7 answer is to import them directly into the reset layer. Their styles are instantly neutralised by layer priority and you never touch their selectors.

The problem with third-party CSS

A typical vendor stylesheet is 2000 lines of unlayered rules with .vendor-button.vendor-button--primary selectors and a liberal sprinkling of !important. Dropped into an ordinary project, it does exactly what its authors intended: it overrides your styles. In a layered project, that is upside down — you want your components to override the vendor's defaults, not the reverse.

The pattern: import at reset level

A single line is the entire technique.

/* In your main.css, before any other layer */
@import url("./vendor/normalize.css") layer(reset);
@import url("./vendor/flatpickr.css") layer(reset);
@import url("./vendor/chartjs.css") layer(reset);

@layer reset, base, layout, components, utilities;

@layer reset {
  /* Your own reset rules go here — in the same layer */
}

The layer(reset) argument on @import places the imported file into the reset layer as if you had typed every rule inside an @layer reset { ... } block. Every vendor rule now lives at the bottom of the cascade. Your base layer, your components, your utilities — all of them win, even against the vendor's !important.

Wait — !important?

This is the part that surprises people. Inside @layer, the importance ordering flips. For normal declarations, higher layers beat lower layers. For !important declarations, lower layers beat higher layers. That sounds wrong until you think about what it is designed to solve: exactly this case. A third-party rule with !important in a low layer is quietly outranked by your higher-layer rules, !important or not.

Why this is the right default

You do not trust third-party CSS enough to let it override your design. But you also do not want to fork or patch it, because that creates a maintenance burden at every version bump. Importing into the reset layer is the compromise: you get the vendor's structural CSS for free, and the vendor's opinions are automatically silenced by your own.

When to pick a higher layer instead

If the vendor stylesheet is genuinely the visual language of the component — a rich-text editor whose toolbar is the product — import it into components instead of reset. The rule of thumb: import into the layer where you would have written the rules yourself if the vendor had handed you a plain list of selectors. Never import into utilities unless you are sure.

One file, one layer

Keep it to one layer per import. Do not attempt to scatter a vendor's rules across multiple layers using @scope or selector rewriting — that is exactly the kind of maintenance burden the pattern exists to avoid.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Move the vendor block out of @layer reset and into a plain unlayered rule. The button turns red — unlayered !important beats layered normal rules. That is the pre-PE7 world. Move it back. Indigo wins. The architecture did the work, not the developer.
