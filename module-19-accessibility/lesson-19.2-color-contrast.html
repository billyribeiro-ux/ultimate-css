Lesson 19.2 · Inclusive Design

Color Contrast: The Math and the Tools

1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some form of color vision deficiency. Sufficient contrast is not a nicety — it is the difference between readable and invisible. WCAG gives us exact ratios; OKLCH gives us intuitive tools to hit them.

WCAG contrast levels

AA (minimum): 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (18pt / 14pt bold) and UI components. AAA (enhanced): 7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text. Most design systems target AA as the baseline and AAA where feasible.

How contrast is calculated

The WCAG formula uses relative luminance: (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05) where L1 is the lighter color and L2 is the darker. Relative luminance comes from linearized sRGB values. The math is non-trivial in CSS, but OKLCH gives us a shortcut.

OKLCH lightness as a proxy

In OKLCH, the L channel (0% to 100%) is perceptually uniform. If your background is oklch(95% ...) and your text is oklch(25% ...), the 70-point lightness gap almost always exceeds 4.5:1. This is not a formal guarantee, but in practice, a lightness difference of 50+ points passes AA for most hue/chroma combinations, and 60+ points is very safe.

contrast-color() — the future

The contrast-color() function (CSS Color Level 5) will automatically choose a text color that meets a target contrast ratio against a given background. Until browser support matures, OKLCH lightness math is the next best approach.

Common failures

Light gray on white: oklch(75% 0 0) on oklch(100% 0 0) — only about 2.5:1. Colored text on colored backgrounds: matching hue does not ensure contrast. Placeholder text: browsers default to a low-contrast gray that often fails. Always check.
