Lesson 14.25 · Microsoft Fluent Design

Fluent: The Mica Material

Mica is the subtlest of Fluent's materials — no blur, no noise, just a gentle tint derived from the desktop wallpaper's dominant color. It makes every app feel like it belongs to the user's personal environment. In CSS, one hue variable tints the entire window.

Mica vs. Acrylic

Acrylic is a per-element effect: blur, noise, and tint on a sidebar, a dialog, or a toolbar. Mica is an app-level effect: applied once at the root, tinting the entire window surface. Acrylic says "this layer floats above content." Mica says "this app belongs on your desktop." The two complement each other — Mica is the base, Acrylic is the elevation.

How Mica works on Windows

Windows extracts the dominant hue from the user's wallpaper and mixes a very low-chroma version into the app's base surface color. The result is imperceptible at a glance but creates a sense of personalization — an app with a warm wallpaper feels warmer, a cool wallpaper makes it feel cooler.

The CSS simulation

Since CSS cannot access the OS wallpaper, we simulate Mica with a --mica-hue custom property:

:root {
  --mica-hue: 220;
  --mica-surface-light: oklch(98% 0.005 var(--mica-hue));
  --mica-surface-dark:  oklch(12% 0.004 var(--mica-hue));
  --color-surface: light-dark(
    var(--mica-surface-light),
    var(--mica-surface-dark)
  );
}

Why such low chroma?

The chroma values (0.004-0.005 in OKLCH) are barely above zero. This is intentional — Mica's tint should be felt, not seen. At higher chroma, the surface would compete with content colors. At zero chroma, the surface would feel generic. The sweet spot is so subtle that users cannot name the tint when asked, but they notice its absence when it is removed.

Applied once, inherited everywhere

Unlike Acrylic (which needs backdrop-filter on each element), Mica sets a CSS custom property at the root and every surface inherits it. The titlebar, sidebar, content area, and footer all share the same tint. This is architecturally simple and performant — no compositing layers, no blur passes.

PE7 placement

The --mica-hue and derived surface colors are tokens. The app layout structure is components. No animation layer is needed — Mica is static.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Change --mica-hue to 30 (warm sunset wallpaper) — the entire app gains a warm, inviting tone. Then try 150 (forest green) and 300 (purple dusk). Notice how the app's personality shifts while remaining fully readable. Now increase --mica-c-light to 0.03 — suddenly the tint is visible and distracting. Mica's power is in its restraint: just enough chroma to personalize, never enough to interfere.
