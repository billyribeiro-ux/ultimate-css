Lesson 14.22 · Microsoft Fluent Design

Fluent: The Acrylic Material

Acrylic is a translucent material with blur, noise, and saturation boost. It creates depth by revealing the content behind it while the noise texture prevents the "smeared watercolor" look of pure blur. This is how Microsoft signals "floating layer."

What makes acrylic work

Pure backdrop-filter: blur() produces a soft, undefined haze. Acrylic adds two corrections: a noise overlay that re-introduces high-frequency detail the blur removed, and a saturation boost that keeps the blurred content vivid rather than washed out. Together they create a material that feels like frosted glass rather than Vaseline on a lens.

The three layers

  • Blur layer — backdrop-filter: blur(30px) saturate(125%). The 30px blur radius matches the apparent density of Windows 11 acrylic. The saturation boost compensates for chroma loss during Gaussian blur.
  • Tint layer — A semi-transparent background color. Light mode: oklch(98% 0.003 264 / 0.72). Dark mode: oklch(12% 0.010 264 / 0.72). The 72% opacity lets enough background through to feel layered without losing readability.
  • Noise layer — A tiny repeating pattern at very low opacity (2-4%). Microsoft uses a 256x256 noise PNG, but we can approximate it with a CSS gradient pattern or an inline SVG data URI.

Why saturate(125%)?

Gaussian blur averages neighboring pixels, and averaging reduces variance — which in color terms means reduced saturation. A bright red next to a white averages to a desaturated pink. The saturate(125%) counteracts this, pushing the blurred result back toward the original vibrancy. Without it, the acrylic feels dull and lifeless.

The noise texture

The noise overlay serves a perceptual purpose: it adds texture that the blur removed. The human visual system is uncomfortable with perfectly smooth gradients at mid-frequencies — they trigger "uncanny valley" responses because natural materials always have texture. Even at 2% opacity, the noise makes acrylic feel like a physical surface rather than a rendering artifact.

PE7 placement

The acrylic material is a component pattern (it defines a reusable surface treatment). The blur radius and tint opacity are tokens. The noise is a texture utility.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Set --saturation to 100% (no boost) — the sidebar looks washed out. Then set it to 175% — oversaturated and garish. The sweet spot is 120-130%. Next, set --noise-opacity to 0 — the acrylic feels "slippery." Even 2-3% noise is enough to anchor the surface perceptually. Finally, change the tint to var(--tint-dark) for an instant dark-mode acrylic.
