Lesson 14.19 · Google Material Design 3

MD3: Shape Morphing & the FAB

MD3 uses a shape scale from extra-small (4px) to full-round, and shapes morph between states. The Floating Action Button is the signature example: a rounded square that expands into a full-width action sheet while its corners animate.

The MD3 shape scale

Material Design 3 defines six shape sizes: extra-small (4px), small (8px), medium (12px), large (16px), extra-large (28px), and full (9999px). Components reference these tokens rather than hard-coding radii, so an entire app's visual language can shift from sharp to rounded by adjusting the scale.

Shape morphing

When a component changes state, its shape token can change too. A FAB at rest uses the "large" shape (16px radius). When expanded into a sheet, it transitions to "extra-large" (28px) or even "medium" (12px) at full width. The border-radius transition creates a visual sense that the surface is physically stretching.

The emphasized easing curve

MD3 uses cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) for "emphasized" transitions. Compare this to a standard ease-out cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1): the emphasized curve starts slower and ends with a sharper deceleration, creating an organic "overshoot-free but weighty" feel. The first control point at 0.2, 0 keeps the element in place slightly longer before accelerating — this delay gives the user's eye time to track the motion.

Why two different durations?

The border-radius transitions in 300ms while the width transitions in 500ms. Shape perception is faster than size perception — the brain registers a corner change almost instantly but needs more time to track a dimensional change. Staggering durations prevents the animation from feeling mechanical.

PE7 placement

Shape tokens (--shape-sm, --shape-lg) belong in tokens. The FAB component structure is components. The morph transition timing and easing are animations.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Replace the emphasized curve cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) with a standard ease-out and toggle the FAB. Notice the motion feels lighter, less intentional. Then try cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) (MD2's standard curve) — it is subtly snappier at the start. The emphasized curve's delayed start is what gives MD3 its distinctive "considered" feel.
