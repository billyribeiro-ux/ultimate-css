Lesson 14.24 · Microsoft Fluent Design

Fluent: Depth, Shadow & Connected Motion

Fluent uses a 3-tier depth model — on-surface, raised, and floating. Each level combines shadow, Y-translate, and subtle scale. Motion follows "connected animation" principles: elements moving between states maintain identity through continuous transform.

The three depth tiers

Unlike MD3's 6-level tonal system, Fluent's depth model is deliberately simple:

  • On-surface (depth 0) — No shadow, no translate. Flat on the page. Used for most content.
  • Raised (depth 1) — A subtle shadow and 1px upward translate with a barely perceptible scale increase (1.005x). Used for cards on hover, focused controls.
  • Floating (depth 2) — A stronger shadow, 4px upward translate, and 1.01x scale. Used for menus, dialogs, tooltips.

Why translateY and scale together?

In the physical world, an object that rises off a surface does two things: it casts a larger, softer shadow, and it appears slightly larger due to perspective (closer to the eye). The translateY moves the element upward to match the shadow's implied light source. The scale simulates the perspective magnification. Neither effect is large — the scale is sub-percent — but together they trigger the brain's depth perception circuits.

The deceleration curve

Fluent uses cubic-bezier(0.1, 0.9, 0.2, 1) for depth transitions. The second control point at 0.9 causes a slight overshoot — the element rises past its target and settles back. This simulates the physical behavior of a damped spring: the card "bounces" imperceptibly when it reaches its raised position, creating a natural settling feel.

Compared to MD3's emphasized curve

MD3 uses cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1) — no overshoot, a delayed start, and a sharp deceleration. Fluent's curve starts immediately and overshoots slightly. The difference reflects design philosophy: MD3 wants "considered, deliberate" motion; Fluent wants "responsive, physical" motion. Neither is better — they serve different brand languages.

Connected motion

When an element transitions between states, Fluent preserves visual continuity by animating all visual properties simultaneously. A card elevating on hover transitions transform, box-shadow, and background-color in lockstep. No property should arrive early or late — synchronization is what makes the motion feel like a single physical change rather than multiple independent animations.

PE7 placement

Depth variables (--depth-0 through --depth-2) are tokens. The card component that references them is components. The transition timing and easing are animations.

Mini-exercise

Replace var(--fluent-decel) with var(--md3-emphasized) on the card hover transition and notice the different feel — the cards now pause before moving, which feels less responsive. Then try removing the scale from the depth transforms, keeping only translateY — the cards feel flatter because the perspective illusion is lost. Finally, increase --depth-2-transform to translateY(-8px) scale(1.03) — too much, and the card floats unnaturally.
