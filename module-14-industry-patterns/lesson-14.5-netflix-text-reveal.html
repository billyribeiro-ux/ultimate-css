Lesson 14.5

Netflix: The Cinematic Text Reveal

Netflix title cards animate in word-by-word with a staggered fade-up and blur clear. Each word enters slightly after the previous one, creating a cascading reveal that feels cinematic. The stagger uses animation-delay calculated per word, and the blur clears from 4px to 0.

What Netflix does

When a Netflix title card or hero headline appears, the text does not simply pop in. Each word fades up from below with a slight vertical offset, a gentle blur that clears as the word settles, and a stagger delay that makes each successive word arrive slightly later. The overall effect takes less than a second but feels deliberate and polished.

The stagger mechanism

Each word is wrapped in a <span>. The stagger delay is calculated using CSS: animation-delay: calc(var(--i) * 80ms) where --i is set inline or via sibling-index() in supporting browsers. The 80ms interval is the sweet spot — fast enough to feel like a single motion, slow enough for the eye to perceive the cascade.

In browsers supporting sibling-index(), the stagger is fully automatic: animation-delay: calc((sibling-index() - 1) * 80ms). No inline styles needed.

The blur-to-clear effect

Each word starts with filter: blur(4px) and transitions to filter: blur(0). This creates a "focusing" effect — the word appears to come into focus as it arrives. Combined with the upward motion (translateY(1rem) to translateY(0)), it suggests the text is emerging from a cinematic depth of field.

The @starting-style approach

For elements entering the DOM dynamically (like a title card that appears after navigation), @starting-style defines the initial hidden state. The browser transitions from @starting-style to the element's actual computed styles, creating a smooth entry without keyframe animations.

Fallback strategy

In browsers without sibling-index() support, the words simply appear instantly — fully visible, no animation. This is the correct progressive enhancement: the content is never hidden in an unsupported browser.

Mini-exercise

Change the stagger interval from 80ms to 200ms. The reveal now feels like a typewriter — too slow, too deliberate. Change it to 30ms and the words all appear nearly simultaneously, losing the cascade feel. Find the interval that feels "cinematic" to your eye. Netflix lands around 60-100ms depending on the headline length.
