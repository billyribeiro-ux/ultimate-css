Lesson 14.4

Netflix: The Hover-Expand Card

Netflix's browse UI shows thumbnail cards that expand on hover — scaling up, shifting to reveal more info, and elevating above neighboring cards with a shadow. The trick is transform: scale() with careful transform-origin and z-index management.

What Netflix does

In the Netflix browse view, show thumbnails sit in horizontal rows. When you hover a card, it scales up to roughly 1.4x its original size, a description overlay appears below, and the card elevates above its siblings with a shadow. Cards at the left edge expand rightward; cards at the right edge expand leftward. Center cards expand from the center.

The transform-origin trick

transform: scale(1.4) scales from the center by default. But edge cards would get clipped by the container's overflow: hidden. Netflix solves this with transform-origin: left on :first-child and transform-origin: right on :last-child. This pushes the expansion away from the edge.

The z-index elevation

A scaled card that stays at its default z-index would be clipped by adjacent cards. On hover, the card's z-index jumps to a value above its siblings, allowing it to visually overlap them. The transition on z-index is intentionally instant (no easing) — you want the elevation to happen immediately, not gradually.

The transition curve

Netflix uses a custom cubic-bezier for the scale and shadow transitions: roughly cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1) — a standard Material Design ease-in-out. The duration is around 300ms. Fast enough to feel responsive, slow enough to feel smooth.

The info reveal

Below the scaled thumbnail, a description panel slides down. This uses a combination of opacity and translate transitions with a slight delay — the scale starts first, the info appears 100ms later. This staggered timing prevents the animation from feeling like everything happens at once.

Accessibility considerations

Hover effects must have keyboard equivalents. A keyboard user tabbing through the cards should see the same expansion and info reveal that a mouse user gets. Add :focus-visible alongside every :hover rule so that focusing a card with the keyboard triggers the same scale(), z-index, and info panel transitions.

The expanded info panel must be accessible to screen readers. Decorative overlays (poster gradients, shadows) should use aria-hidden="true" so assistive technology skips them and reads only the meaningful content inside the info panel.

On touch devices, :hover does not exist in any reliable way. The info should either be visible by default (no hover gate), or the card should be wrapped in a <button> or <a> so a tap triggers :focus and reveals the content. Hiding essential information behind hover alone makes it unreachable on mobile.

Mini-exercise

Change scale(1.4) to scale(1.8) and watch how the card overwhelms its neighbors. Then change the transition duration from 300ms to 150ms — notice how it feels snappier but less polished. Netflix chose 300ms for a reason: it gives the eye time to track the motion without feeling sluggish.
