Lesson 14.1

Apple: The Parallax Hero Scroll

Apple.com product pages create cinematic depth by scrolling the background image at a different rate than the foreground text. The old way was background-attachment: fixed. The modern way uses scroll-driven animations with different speed multipliers per layer.

What Apple does

Visit any Apple product page — iPhone, MacBook, Vision Pro. The hero section features a large product image that moves slower than the headline text as you scroll. This parallax creates a sense of depth: the background feels farther away, the text feels closer. It is a cinematic trick borrowed from film, where layers at different distances move at different apparent speeds.

The old way: background-attachment fixed

background-attachment: fixed pins a background image to the viewport rather than the element. It creates a crude parallax but causes severe performance problems — the browser must repaint the background on every scroll frame, and it fails entirely on iOS Safari. This approach is deprecated in practice.

The modern way: scroll-driven animations

The modern technique uses animation-timeline: scroll() to bind CSS animations to the scroll position of the nearest scroll container. By giving the background layer a smaller range of motion (say, 0 to -20%) and the foreground text a larger range (0 to -40%), the background appears to move slower. The depth differential between those two rates is what creates the parallax illusion.

The key properties are:

  • animation-timeline: scroll() — binds the animation to scroll position instead of time.
  • animation-range: entry 0% exit 100% — defines which portion of the scroll drives the animation.
  • Different translateY distances per layer — the speed multiplier that creates depth.

The @supports fallback

Browsers without animation-timeline support see a static hero — no parallax, but fully functional. The @supports (animation-timeline: scroll()) block ensures the scroll-driven styles only apply when the browser can handle them. This is progressive enhancement at its best: the baseline is solid, the upgrade is cinematic.

Why this matters at the PE7 level

A junior developer copies a parallax library. A senior developer uses background-attachment: fixed. A principal engineer understands that parallax is a depth differential between layer speeds, implements it with scroll-driven animations for 60fps GPU-composited performance, and wraps it in @supports with a reduced-motion fallback. That is the difference.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Change the background translateY from -20% to -5% and the foreground from -40% to -60%. Notice how the depth illusion intensifies. Then try making them equal — the parallax vanishes. The effect is entirely about the differential between layer speeds, not the absolute speed of either layer.
