Lesson 14.3

Apple: The Frosted Glass Nav

Apple's navigation bar uses a translucent frosted glass effect — content behind bleeds through with a blur, and the nav becomes more opaque as you scroll. This is backdrop-filter: blur() combined with scroll-driven opacity interpolation.

What Apple does

Apple's sticky nav starts nearly transparent. As you scroll down, the background becomes progressively more opaque until it reaches near-solid at about 200px of scroll. The frosted glass effect — where content behind the nav is visible but blurred — gives the interface a premium, layered feel.

The backdrop-filter foundation

backdrop-filter: blur(12px) applies a Gaussian blur to everything behind the element. Combined with a semi-transparent background, this creates the frosted glass illusion. The key is using color-mix(in oklch, var(--surface) 80%, transparent) for the background — this gives you precise control over the opacity while keeping the color in the OKLCH perceptual space.

Scroll-dependent opacity

The progressive opacity increase uses a scroll-driven animation that interpolates the background from 60% opacity to 95% opacity as the user scrolls from 0 to 200px. This can be achieved with animation-timeline: scroll() and a @keyframes that transitions a registered @property --nav-opacity custom property.

An alternative approach in newer browsers is @container scroll-state(stuck: top), which applies styles only when a sticky element is actually stuck. This is a cleaner binary toggle rather than a smooth interpolation.

Performance considerations

backdrop-filter is GPU-accelerated in all modern browsers, but it can cause performance issues when applied to large areas or when combined with complex compositing. For a nav bar — a thin strip at the top — performance is excellent. For a full-screen overlay, test carefully.

Demo — frosted glass nav over colorful content

Scroll to see the frosted glass

As you scroll, notice how the colorful hero content bleeds through the nav with a blur effect. The nav maintains readability while letting the vibrancy of the content show through. This is the hallmark of Apple's design language — layers of translucency that create depth without sacrificing clarity.

The frosted glass effect combines backdrop-filter with a semi-transparent background. The blur radius controls how much detail comes through. Too little blur and the text behind is distracting. Too much and you lose the layered effect entirely.

Try it yourself

Mini-exercise

Change the backdrop-filter: blur(12px) to blur(2px) and observe how much more detail bleeds through. Then change the background opacity from 0.7 to 0.95 — the frost effect nearly vanishes. Find the sweet spot for your eye: the right balance between readability and the frosted depth illusion.
